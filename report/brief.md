# Operations Planning Brief: Weekly Warehouse Flex Staffing Under Order-Line Uncertainty

**Prepared by:** Industrial Engineering & Operations Research Analytics  
**Scope:** Weekly Friday-origin allocation of 40 flexible labor hours (ten indivisible 4-hour blocks) across Monday–Friday picking operations  
**Data Source & Epistemic Boundary:** [UCI Online Retail II (Dataset ID 502)](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/502/online+retail+ii) ([DOI: 10.24432/C5CG6D](https://doi.org/10.24432/C5CG6D), CC BY 4.0) provides historical retail invoice lines used strictly as **proxies for potential order-picking demand**. Actual warehouse pick rates, travel batching, floor staffing, and physical queue lengths are unobserved; capacity parameters ($C, q$) and backlog recursions are explicitly synthetic operational models.

## 1. Operational Decision Problem
Every Friday afternoon, fulfillment leadership must lock next week's flexible staffing roster—ten indivisible 4-hour labor blocks ($40\text{ hours/week}$, capped at $0 \le x_d \le 4\text{ blocks/weekday}$)—before Monday–Friday orders arrive. Flat scheduling (`2-2-2-2-2` blocks/day) ignores predictable Monday weekend-order accumulation, Q4 seasonal ramps, and UK bank-holiday troughs, whereas naive optimization against noisy forecasts risks starving Thursday–Friday shifts and triggering expensive weekend SLA rollovers.

## 2. Key Analytical Findings (Predict-Then-Optimize Dynamics)
1. **Forecast Regularization Protects Against Bang-Bang Misallocation:** In out-of-sample rolling validation and 12-week sealed backtesting on the repository's two-sheet verification benchmark (`results/verification_smoke/`, $C=36\text{ lines/day}$, $q=8\text{ lines/block}$, $\lambda_{\text{Fri}}=3$), nonnegative L2-regularized Ridge regression (1-, 4-, and 52-week lags + weekday and UK statutory holiday features) cut sealed-test WAPE from **38.46%** (Previous-Week baseline) to **29.45%**.
2. **Better Forecasts Do Not Automatically Guarantee Better Schedules Every Week:** Driving the five-day [Pyomo](https://pyomo.readthedocs.io/en/6.8.0/library_reference/appsi/appsi.html)/HiGHS MILP with noisy Previous-Week forecasts reduced raw intra-week line-days ($2{,}248$ vs. $2{,}515$ Flat) **but degraded total Friday-penalized objective cost by $-1.02\%$** ($4{,}741$ vs. $4{,}693$ Flat; losing 5 of 12 weeks). When a noisy forecast over-predicts Monday/Tuesday on a near-balanced week, front-loaded flex blocks hit the `max(0, .)` empty-queue floor mid-week—wasting capacity that cannot be banked—and leave Friday exposed to a $4\times$ carryover weight ($1 + \lambda_{\text{Fri}} = 4$).
3. **Regularized Ridge + MILP Maintains Positive Gain Across All 9 Sensitivity Regimes:** Pairing Ridge forecasts with the MILP reduced total objective cost to **$4{,}558$** (**$+2.88\%$ vs. Flat Staffing**, **$+3.86\%$ vs. Previous-Week + MILP**, capturing **15.6%** of the theoretical perfect-foresight Oracle bound of $3{,}826$; winning 7 weeks, tying 1, and losing 4). Across the $3 \times 3$ sensitivity grid ($q \times \{0.75, 1.00, 1.25\}$, $\lambda_{\text{Fri}} \in \{1, 3, 5\}$), Ridge + MILP remained positive in **all 9 scenarios** ($+1.6\%$ to $+11.0\%$), whereas Previous-Week + MILP deteriorated to **$-12.2\%$** when higher block productivity ($1.25q$) increased mid-week queue-emptying frequency.

## 3. Practical Recommendations & Guardrails
* **Adopt Regularized Multi-Lag Forecasting Before Activating Shift Optimization:** Never couple an unregularized 1-week lag forecast directly to a deterministic MILP; shrinkage toward seasonal/weekday baselines damps false bang-bang front-loading.
* **Enforce a Late-Week Risk Guardrail on Balanced Weeks:** In weeks where forecasted weekly demand sits within $\pm 10\%$ of total weekly capacity ($5C + 10q$), enforce a minimum Thursday+Friday reserve of $\ge 2$ flex blocks (or solve a two-stage stochastic / CVaR MILP over historical residual scenarios) to cap Friday rollover exposure during unexpected late-week wholesale bursts.
* **Instrument WMS Time Studies Before Production Deployment:** Before transitioning from synthetic calibration ($C = 0.70\,\text{median}$, $q = 0.15\,\text{median}$) to live labor rostering, log WMS scan-to-scan pick timestamps, lines-per-labor-hour by zone/cube, and Friday order-cutoff spillover across a 4-week shadow pilot, and execute `bash scripts/reproduce.sh` on a networked host to review empirical [UCI Online Retail II](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/502/online+retail+ii) metrics in `results/uci/`.
